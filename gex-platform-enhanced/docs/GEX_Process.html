The GEX Process

How a green-fuel project becomes bankable on GreenEarthX: what is asked, who supplies it, who checks it, and what each step makes possible. For producers, offtakers, financiers, insurers, certifiers and verifiers.

Projects don't become bankable because the story is convincing. They become bankable when the evidence converges.

The path of one evidence item

  1. Requested. Each item is assigned to a named person at the company that owns the work, with what it blocks.
  2. Submitted. The document is uploaded and its SHA-256 recorded; the submitter declares the instrument's maturity. A new document returns the item to submitted: what was verified was the earlier file.
  3. Verified. By someone independent of the submitter and of the project owner — the contract's counterparty for a contract, an appointed independent reviewer for everything else. Verification names the document on file.
  4. Scored. The engine counts an item only when it is verified at or above the maturity its gate requires.
  5. Judged. Each gate gets one answer (below); satisfied gates set the project's state.
  6. Made eligible. A state makes a capital decision eligible. The capital provider still decides.

Maturity

Verification answers whether a document is authentic. Maturity answers how far the instrument behind it has progressed — a letter of intent can be perfectly authentic. The submitter declares maturity; a reviewer may confirm or lower it, never raise it. An instrument with no declared maturity does not count.

  • INTENT Plan, mandate, letter of intent, MOU, strategy, or application in preparation.
  • INDICATIVE Term sheet, quotation, indication of terms, or application submitted.
  • AGREED Agreed-form document with all terms settled, not yet signed or granted.
  • EXECUTED Signed, bound, granted, issued or passed.
  • EFFECTIVE In force: conditions precedent satisfied, policy incepted, appeal period expired.

Who verifies

GEX verifies nothing. A contract is verified by its counterparty's confirmation on the contract's token; an independent reviewer may also take part, but is not required. Studies, analyses, opinions, audits and the producer's own plan need an independent reviewer, appointed by GEX staff on the instruction of a named party. Each reviewer role covers fixed gates:

A gate's answer

SATISFIED
Every applicable item verified at its maturity, every required commitment reached, every cross-contract check passed.
BLOCKED
A deal-killer is missing, a commitment is below the level the gate needs, a cross-contract check failed, or an event undermines the gate.
UNRESOLVED
The items are verified, but a cross-contract check cannot run because confirmed terms are missing. Missing data is never a pass.
INCOMPLETE
Items are still to be verified.
WAIVED
Not required for this financing model. Counts toward the ladder; never shown as satisfied by evidence.

Project premises

Two premises decide which evidence applies. The financing model: balance-sheet funding drops the items that only protect an external lender and waives the gates made only of them (G6, G8). The power model (grid-connected, off-grid behind the meter, hybrid): each drops the items that belong to the others.

A change that removes evidence takes effect only when its supporting document is registered and a second party confirms it — another party to the project (a funder, the counterparty) or GEX staff, never the company that proposed it. Any party may register a document contesting a premise; while it is contested the protective premise applies (project finance, every power item), until GEX staff decide. A change that adds evidence back applies at once.

Contract terms and the contract token

Documents can each satisfy a gate and still contradict one another. So the terms that matter — volumes, tenors, dates, specifications, damages caps — are recorded once, and the engine compares them across contracts. Every contract has a token: an append-only, hash-chained record of its versions and confirmations. A change to the terms is a new version on the token. Only terms in the latest version confirmed by the other party reach the engine; until then the checks that need them read UNRESOLVED.

What moves a project backwards

  • A verified item replaced by a new document, rejected, or withdrawn.
  • A cross-contract check that fails, or a term change not yet confirmed.
  • A counterparty or reviewer disputing terms it had confirmed.
  • A premise contested or rejected.
  • An adverse event: insolvency, revoked power or support, suspended certification.

Each names the gate responsible, and the capital eligibility it carried is withdrawn.

Re-evaluation, reports and who sees what

Every change that can move a gate — evidence, a document, a premise, a contract term, a confirmation — re-evaluates the project at once. When the state or a gate's answer changes, the project's designated executives are notified; by default the owner's executives. Each project has a report showing its state, the share of evidence verified, and every open item side by side — evidence to verify, commitments not reached, contracts that disagree, data missing — each with its owner, due date and what it holds back.

Each party sees the gates that concern its role. The project's state and overall figure always cover every gate, and the screen says so when a viewer sees fewer.

What GEX never does

  • Verify a party's evidence, or confirm a party's terms.
  • Commit, release or draw capital. A state makes a decision eligible; the capital provider decides.
  • Let a gate stand in for a lender's, investor's or grant authority's own approval.
  • Show a figure the engine did not compute from verified evidence.
